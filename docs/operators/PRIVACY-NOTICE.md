# Privacy notice template for Faro instances

> **This is not legal advice.** It is a starting point written by the Faro project, which is not your lawyer and doesn't know your organisation or the laws that apply to you. **Have a lawyer check your finished notice before you publish it.** You, the operator, are responsible for what it says and for following it.

**Who this is for:** the person or organisation that runs a Faro instance (the **operator**): a church, a college, a small group or one person. Fill it in, have it checked, and publish it.

## How to use this template

1. **Read the whole template once.** Everything below the line "The notice starts here" is the notice itself, written to the people who use your instance. Everything above it is for you; leave it out when you publish.
2. **Fill in every placeholder.** There are two kinds:
   - **[FILL IN: …]** is something only you know: your name, your contact details, your settings, your providers.
   - **[ASK YOUR LAWYER: …]** is a legal decision. Don't guess at these.

   Search for `[FILL IN` and `[ASK YOUR LAWYER` before you publish. None should be left.
3. **Copy your real settings.** The retention periods in section 6 must match your instance. Find them in the admin console under **Settings**, or in the `settings` line of `faro-server doctor` (for example `retention 90/30/90 days (headers/blobs/alerts)`).
4. **Remove what doesn't apply.** Section 12 (GDPR) is for instances that serve people in the European Economic Area or the UK. Section 13 (FERPA) is for US colleges and universities. If you leave one out, ask your lawyer whether other laws apply instead.
5. **Publish it** where people can read it before they sign up, for example on your organisation's website, and give people the link when you invite them. Faro can't show this document itself yet. [FILL IN: where you publish it.]
6. **Keep it up to date.** Change it when you change your retention settings, your email or hosting provider, or your backups, and when a new Faro version changes what it stores.

### How this relates to your instance's built-in notice

Every Faro instance already has a short notice at `/privacy` on its own address (for example `https://faro.yourchurch.org/privacy`). Every Faro page links to it ("What this instance can see"), and the admin console's overview shows it too. Faro generates it from your instance's name and live settings, so it is always accurate about what the software does. You can read it as plain text with `curl -s https://faro.yourchurch.org/v1/privacy-notice`.

The built-in notice says what Faro does. It can't say who you are, why you run the instance, how to contact you, what your providers do, how long you keep backups, or what the law gives people. This template covers those. The two must agree: if you change a setting, check both. In your published notice, point people to the built-in page (section 1 does this).

### Where the facts come from

Every statement below about what Faro collects, keeps, shows or deletes comes from the Faro source code and its documentation: [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md) §4.3, §5.3, §5.4, §8 and §12, [PROTOCOL.md](../PROTOCOL.md), [SELF-HOSTING.md](../SELF-HOSTING.md) and the server code (`server/src`). It describes the Faro version this file came with. If you changed Faro's code, or run a much newer or older version, check it again.

## 2. What Faro is

Faro is an accountability tool. A person (a **member**) chooses to install it on their own computer and invites people they trust (their **allies**). Faro looks at the member's screen on the member's computer. When it flags something, or when Faro is switched off or interfered with, it tells the member's allies.

Faro is not a surveillance tool. We can't use it to monitor anyone who hasn't chosen it. We can't install it on anyone's computer, add ourselves as anyone's ally, or read anyone's reports.

## 3. What we can't see

Reports and alerts are **end-to-end encrypted**. They are locked on the member's computer, before they reach our server, so that only the member and the allies the member approved can unlock them. They unlock them in their own web browser, with their own passphrase. Our server never has the keys.

So we **cannot** see:

- **Screenshots.** Full screenshots never leave the member's computer, and are never saved anywhere, not even there. Faro keeps only small, heavily blurred pictures of flagged moments, and only in encrypted form. Nobody can un-blur them.
- **What reports and alerts say:** ratings, the reasons for a flag, websites, apps, window titles, which account on the computer was in use, and the blurred pictures.
- **Passphrases, recovery codes and private keys.** They are locked in your browser before they are sent to us, and we can't unlock them. That is also why we can't reset a forgotten passphrase.

We also can't add a key that members' computers would encrypt to. Computers only encrypt to keys the member has approved.

**One honest limit.** The Faro web pages you use come from our server. If someone who runs the server changed those pages on purpose, they could in principle capture a passphrase when you type it. We won't do that. A separate desktop app for allies, which wouldn't depend on our server for this, is planned by the Faro project.

## 5. The emails we send

Faro sends email for sign-in links, invitations and notifications. Emails never contain what a report or alert says. They contain:

- **Notifications:** the member's name, the computer's name and whether it is urgent. For example: "Urgent: new alert for Sam on Laptop-X. Open Faro to view." The hourly digest gives counts per member.
- **Account changes:** when an account is disabled, re-enabled, being deleted, or a deletion is cancelled, the email says so in words, names the people concerned and gives the dates.
- **Invitations:** the name of the person who invited you and our instance's name.
- **Sign-in links:** a link that works once, for 15 minutes.

We send email through [FILL IN: your email provider, for example Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Mailgun or Amazon SES]. Like any email provider, it handles the addresses, subject lines and text of these emails. [FILL IN: how long your provider keeps them, if you know.]

## 8. Deleting your account

Anyone can delete their own account: members, allies and admins.

1. Sign in. Faro asks you to have signed in within the last 10 minutes; it offers to send you a sign-in link.
2. Open **Settings** (your name, top right) and use **Delete your account** at the bottom. Type your email address to confirm.

What happens next:

- **Faro tells people straight away, by email.** If you are a member, your allies are told. If you are an ally, the members you support and their other allies are told. These emails can't be switched off. This is on purpose: leaving Faro is allowed, but never silent.
- **Nothing else changes for 7 days.** Your computers keep reporting and your allies keep getting alerts. You can still sign in, and every page has a **Cancel deletion** button. If you cancel, the same people are told. While you wait, you can't invite allies or add computers.
- **Then everything about you is deleted** from the server: your account, computers, activity, alerts, reports, keys, allies and settings. Faro waits until every deletion email has been sent, so if our email isn't working, the deletion waits too. From then on the server refuses your computers, so uninstall Faro from them.

**What remains after deletion:**

- your internal account code (for example `usr_…`) and public keys inside other members' signed records, because signed records can't be edited. They don't include your name or email address;
- short codes naming your internal account code on other members' alerts (for example an alert that an ally was removed);
- a scrambled form of your computers' access codes, for 365 days, so that your computers get a clear "account deleted" answer;
- server log lines with your internal account code, until the logs are rotated (section 4);
- copies in our backups, until those backups are deleted (section 6).

**If you can't delete your account yourself**, for example because your account is disabled or you can no longer get email at your address, contact us (section 1). [FILL IN: how you check that the request is really from you.] An admin can then delete it for you. It works the same way as deleting it yourself:

- You and everyone in an accountability partnership with you are emailed straight away. The emails say your account is being deleted by the instance administrator. They don't name the admin. If your account is disabled, you are emailed too.
- Nothing else changes for 7 days. If you can still sign in, every page says the instance administrator asked for the deletion and has a **Cancel deletion** button. Any of our admins can cancel too, for example if you ask us to. If anyone cancels, the same people are told.
- Then everything about you is deleted, as above, and the same things remain.

Our admins can't delete their own account this way, and the instance's last admin can't be deleted. Every admin deletion, and every cancellation, is recorded in our admin log with which admin did it, and emailed to all our admins. We keep that log. Once your account is deleted, it no longer names you. [FILL IN: anything else you will do, for example how long you take to act on a request. ASK YOUR LAWYER: whether that is fast enough.]
